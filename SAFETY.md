# Clinical safety

## Purpose and boundary

OpenScribe transcribes and arranges supplied information into drafts for a
clinician. It does not diagnose, triage, recommend treatment, make clinical
decisions, or act autonomously. A clinician must review, amend as needed, and
approve every draft before it is committed to the clinical record.

## Safety management

MemRe Ltd is the manufacturer. OpenScribe has MHRA Class I registration 37281,
registered on 27 January 2026. Dr Cameron Nicholson is the Clinical Safety
Officer. Contact: [clinical-safety@openscribe.co.uk](mailto:clinical-safety@openscribe.co.uk).

MemRe Ltd maintains an approved DCB0129 Clinical Safety Case Report for
OpenScribe v1.0, dated 7 May 2026. This is not external certification. A later
release needs a recorded Clinical Safety Officer safety-impact review before it
can be described as covered by that case.

## Main hazards and controls

Drafts can contain transcription or summarisation errors, omissions, the wrong
patient context, unsafe user instructions, or no output when the service is
unavailable. Clinician review before committing a draft is the main control.
The controlled hazard log, including detailed controls and residual risks, is
available on request from [clinical-safety@openscribe.co.uk](mailto:clinical-safety@openscribe.co.uk).

## Deploying organisations

Each NHS organisation deploying OpenScribe remains responsible for its local
DCB0160 assessment, DPIA, workflow validation, staff training, and monitoring
and incident processes.
